## Load check (pilot go-live checklist, 2026-11-22)
**What was done.** `tools/load-check.ts` (`check:load`): a throwaway PostgreSQL with about 30 times a pilot's data (30 communities, 400 vendors, 6,000 people, 30,000 requests, 300,000 audit rows), real registration and two-step sign-in, 25 screens timed over HTTP with row-level security on, one caller and then eight at once.
**What it found.** The platform-wide audit trail (median 2.7 s, p95 6.9 s) and the operations request queue (median 0.34 s, p95 1.3 s) read and sorted every row, because both list newest-first across all communities and no index started with the date. Fixed by migration 0017 (two indexes, no policy or grant change): 55 ms and 60 ms. A test fails if either index is missing.
**Result.** Measured on the development machine (one machine runs the database, the API and the client; rate limits raised so they do not hide the database), 30 communities, 400 vendors with 1,600 offerings, 6,000 people, 30,000 requests, 300,000 audit rows, about 30 times a pilot. One caller at a time, p95: every family, community and vendor screen 35 to 90 ms (catalog with every filter 60 to 90 ms), the operations queue 38 to 41 ms, the audit trail 24 ms, writing a request 52 ms and a report 35 ms. Eight at once, p95: nothing over 130 ms except sign-in (256 ms, password hashing), the operations summary (1,044 ms) and the operations "waiting for me" check (418 ms).
**Honest limits.** Not a capacity test (one machine, no network, no browser, rate limits raised). Two operations screens still read every request and grow with their number (about 0.3 s at 30,000): fine for a pilot, replace with trigger-maintained counts before about 100,000 requests. Not run on a host.

## Safety concerns channel (Phase 6.4): verification and gate report (for founder approval)
**Why this was built now (2026-11-22).** A hard review of the launch document found a claim I had written that the product did not support: "anyone can tell the platform team" about a safety concern. `SECURITY.md` section 3 has required a "reporting channel for safety concerns with a documented response process" since Phase 1, and `MASTER_PROMPT.md` ranks child safety first, yet nothing in the product or in this file's gap lists provided one, and no email carries a Reply-To. A platform that introduces strangers to children cannot launch without a way to say "this worried me". It is an existing requirement, not new scope, so no item is displaced. It is numbered 6.4 because it completes the Phase 5 and 6 loop (the people in it), not the Discovery catalog.

**Built (migration 0016, API, web):**
- `concerns`: a report with a category (a child's safety, how someone behaved, money, other), an optional vendor it is about, a subject, the details, a status that only moves forward (open, reviewing, resolved, closed without action), and an outcome the reporter reads. Readable only by the reporter and by platform staff with `concerns.manage`; there is deliberately no vendor, community or same-community clause (the vendor, or a community administrator, may be the subject). Permissions are data: `concerns.create` for family, community and vendor roles, `concerns.manage` for operations, platform administrators, customer support and the super administrator. A trigger fixes what a report says, enforces forward-only status and a required outcome, and records who decided; a second trigger caps ten undecided reports per person.
- API: `POST /concerns`, `GET /concerns/mine`, `GET /concerns` (the team's queue, child safety first), `PUT /concerns/:id/status`. The desk is emailed through `OPS_ALERT_EMAIL` that something was reported (never what); the reporter is emailed that there is an update (never the outcome); the audit record carries the category only; the team's "Waiting for you" counts child-safety and other reports separately.
- Web: **Report a concern** (also linked from every vendor page, which carries the vendor into the form) under the emergency numbers 112 and 1098 (Childline), with a plain promise about who sees the report, and the reporter's own list with status in words and the outcome; **Concerns** for the team (child safety first, who reported and from where, take on, decide with words, confirmation after deciding).

**Verified (run for real on 2026-11-22):** see `TEST_PLAN.md` for the counts and the mutation results (32 of 34 caught; the other two are one redundant pair, and three weak tests found by escapes were strengthened). The browser journey also found and fixed a UX gap (a decided report vanished with no confirmation).

**Known gaps (concerns channel):**
- A person with no community or vendor selected cannot use the in-app form (the page tells them to choose where they are acting). Added afterwards: a public **Safety** page (no sign-in, in every footer) with the emergency numbers and the team's email and phone from `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPPORT_EMAIL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPPORT_PHONE`; **the founder must set them before launch** (`LAUNCH_READINESS.md` B11), and there is still no chat and no Reply-To on any email.
- Nothing escalates an unanswered report (no reminder, no second recipient): the queue now shows how long each has waited and calls out a child-safety report that has waited a day or more, and the desk is emailed once. A named person on duty is a founder decision (`LAUNCH_READINESS.md` B8).
- A report has no private notes of its own, no attachments, and the reporter cannot add to it after sending; findings go in the vendor's internal notes.
- The wording shown to families (emergency numbers, who can see the report) and whether the platform has a duty to report to the authorities (for example under the POCSO Act) are for legal review.
- Not covered: Docker, CI on a host, Firefox, Safari, a screen reader.

**Gate question for the founder:** accept the concerns channel as built? Two decisions only you can make before launch: who is on duty to read the queue, and how fast (suggestion: child safety the same day).

## Phase 7 (Discovery catalog): first slice, verification and gate report (for founder approval)
**Scope decision (2026-11-22).** `MASTER_PROMPT.md` section 8 defines Phase 7 as the *Discovery catalog* (catalog, age and interest filters, recommendations, community events listing) and its recommended MVP includes "a minimal slice of 7"; `MASTER_ROADMAP.md` places the full phase after pilot data. The catalog itself (browse, search, category, age, price model) already existed from Phase 4. This slice adds only what the requirements call for and the pilot can use now, without waiting for data: **FR-DC-2** price and rating filters, vendor ratings on every card, and **FR-DC-3** saved offerings ("favorites"). It is inside the MVP boundary, so no item is displaced. Deliberately NOT built, and why: **FR-DC-4 personalized recommendations** (no data to personalize from; guessing would be noise), **sorting by price or rating** (needs a composite cursor for stable paging; the default stays newest first), **per-community category enablement (FR-DC-1)** (a Must in the requirements, not built; no pilot community has asked to hide a category), **community events listing** (events are Phase 8), **indoor/outdoor filter** (not a stored attribute of an offering).

**Built (migration 0015, API, web):**
- `saved_offerings`: a person's private list, per community. Row-level security shows a person only their own rows in the community they are acting in; no platform or vendor access; insert and delete only (no UPDATE grant); saving needs `catalog.read` and an offering the caller can see right now; a trigger caps 200 per person per community.
- API: `PUT` and `DELETE /catalog/offerings/:id/saved` (idempotent, 204; an unseen offering is 404 exactly like an unknown one; 422 `too_many_saved`); the catalog list gains `minRating`, `savedOnly`, and each item gains `rating { count, average }` (published reviews only) and `saved`; the vendor page marks which offerings are saved.
- Web: ratings in words on cards, filters for highest price, rating and "only the activities I have saved", a Save / Remove from saved button on cards and the vendor page (label says what it will do, polite live confirmation, never disabled mid-request).

**Verified (run for real on 2026-11-22):** see `TEST_PLAN.md` for the counts. New tests: `packages/db/test/saved-offerings.test.ts` (privacy from neighbour, operations and vendor; cannot save drafts, unapproved vendors, unknown ids, for another person, in another community, in platform scope, without the permission; no edits; own deletes only; vanishes without being exposed when unpublished or withdrawn; the 200 cap), tenant-isolation fixture and probe for the new table, the migrations list (15), three API tests (ratings count only published reviews and an unrated vendor never passes a rating filter; the save flow end to end including 404, 403, 401 and the cross-origin refusal; the cap), and one browser journey (save, reload, saved filter, price and rating filters, remove from the vendor page, the empty saved message) plus the existing light and dark axe scans of the catalog (now with a saved card) and the phone catalog check. Mutation check: 17 of 18 backend and 6 of 6 web mutations caught; three weak tests found by escapes were strengthened and the remaining escape (saving a draft) is a documented redundant layer (TEST_PLAN.md). Full run on 2026-11-22: typecheck and lint clean, 549 vitest tests in 21 files, `build:api`, the backup drill, and 106 Playwright tests passed (1 skipped by design).

**Known gaps (Phase 7 first slice):**
- Not built: personalized recommendations, sort options, per-community category enablement, indoor/outdoor, events listing (reasons above).
- The price filter assumes rupees (the pilot is in India; offerings carry a currency but the filter field does not).
- A rating filter silently leaves out vendors with no reviews, as the on-screen hint says; with few reviews in the pilot, this will hide most vendors, so it may be better as a "show rated first" sort later.
- Ratings are per vendor (all communities), not per offering.
- A person's saved list is not included in the family export (there is no account deletion yet); revisit with the legal review.
- Rating is computed per page row by a lateral subquery: fine for a pilot catalog, to be revisited with real volume.
- Docker, CI, Firefox, Safari and screen-reader coverage remain untested, as before.

**Gate question for the founder:** accept this slice and treat Phase 7 as Partially Completed? The rest of Phase 7 waits for pilot data (what families actually search for and save). The pilot go-live checklist above remains the next real step.

## Known limitations and deviations (Phase 2)
| Item | Detail | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| DB trusts `app.tenant_id` | RLS enforces the tenant boundary, but the database does not itself check that the user is a member of the tenant named in context. A bug in tenant resolution could expose that tenant's data. Documented by a test. | **Mitigated in 2.2a:** `resolveTenantState` verifies an active membership on every request (tested: non-member, removed member, forged header). Any new code that calls `withContext` with a tenant must go through it. Consider an in-DB membership check later. |
| Framework changed | NestJS proposal replaced by Fastify | `adr/0004-api-framework-fastify.md` |
| OpenAPI not yet generated | Hand-maintained table in `API_DOCUMENTATION.md` | Generate from zod schemas when endpoints stabilize |
| `packages/contracts` not created | Schemas live beside routes in `apps/api` | Extract when the web app needs shared types |
| Tests use embedded Postgres by default | `packages/db/test/global-setup.ts` starts a throwaway PostgreSQL 16 when `TEST_DATABASE_ADMIN_URL` is unset; CI and Docker set it. Embedded binaries come from the `embedded-postgres` dev dependency. | Run suite against Docker Postgres once the engine works. |
| IDs are random UUIDs | `gen_random_uuid()` (v4), not time-ordered UUIDv7, which PostgreSQL 16 lacks. | Revisit if index locality matters. |
| Role assignments are insert/delete only | No UPDATE grant by design. | None. |
| Docker Desktop | Would not start its engine on this machine in this session. | Investigate separately. |
